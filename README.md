# Measure the Door, Not the Exit

**Five statistics for the AI-and-work debate, and who should collect them**

A policy brief. Exposure indices measure what a model can do to a task bundle; they never observe an employer. This brief proposes five cheap, collectable statistics that would adjudicate the displacement question, plus a six-question survey module for an existing business survey.

[Brief (PDF)](paper/measure-the-door.pdf) · [Markdown](paper/measure-the-door.md)

## The five statistics

| # | Statistic | Unit | What it settles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adoption intensity: AI spend per employee, by task group | Firm | Whether exposure and adoption are even correlated |
| 2 | Entry-level share of hires | Occupation | Whether the adjustment runs through the entry door |
| 3 | Separation type by tenure | Occupation × tenure | Attrition versus dismissal, and at which seniority |
| 4 | Reinstatement rate: displaced tasks re-appearing as hiring requirements | Occupation | The optimistic branch of the task framework |
| 5 | Within-occupation wage dispersion (P90/P10) | Occupation | Whether routine work loses value before headcount moves |

Each is specified with a definition, what it settles, who collects it, and the observation that would refute the displacement thesis. The brief also names what would change my mind: adoption rising while entry-level hiring and wage dispersion stay flat for eight quarters, or reinstatement rates returning to historical norms.

## Why not just employment data

Sector employment and unemployment rates cannot separate AI substitution from the interest-rate cycle and pandemic normalisation. The information sector's 12.2 per cent decline since November 2022 is equally consistent with all three. Companion papers:

- [Exposure Is Not Adoption](https://github.com/dkramer95-stack/ai-exposure-labor-market) — 129 months of BLS data, the information-sector break, five falsifiable predictions.
- [Hires Before Headcount](https://github.com/dkramer95-stack/hires-before-headcount) — the flow mechanism: hires peaked in October 2021, and the sector shrank through attrition, not layoffs.
